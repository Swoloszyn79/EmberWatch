        Understanding EmberWatch

        EmberWatch translates official data into plain language. It never replaces official sources — those are always labelled and linked.

        What is the EmberWatch status?

        A single plain-language read of everything around you: CLEAR (nothing significant), WATCH (conditions changing), CONCERN (something could affect you today), ACT NOW (an official evacuation notice or immediate hazard). It is EmberWatch's own assessment — not a government classification. An official evacuation order or alert always overrides it.

        AQHI vs AQI vs PM2.5

        Canada uses the Air Quality Health Index (AQHI), 1–10+: 1–3 low risk, 4–6 moderate, 7–10 high, 10+ very high. We also show the U.S. AQI (0–500), which is more granular in heavy smoke, and PM2.5 — the fine smoke particles (µg/m³) that matter most during wildfires.

        Nearby Fire Concern

        How much a nearby wildfire could matter to your spot — based on the fire's size, stage of control, whether it's a “fire of note”, its distance, and whether wind is carrying conditions toward you. A small, contained fire close by stays Low; a large out-of-control fire raises it even from farther away. It is not a property-safety guarantee.

        Fire-weather & dryness

        Fires start and spread most easily when fuels are dry — driven by low afternoon humidity, days since rain, heat and wind. BC's official danger classes run Low → Moderate → High → Extreme.

        Fire-danger ratings describe how easily fires may start and spread. Burning restrictions and campfire prohibitions are issued separately by BC Wildfire Service — a High or Extreme rating does not automatically mean a ban is in place. Always check current official restrictions before burning: BC fire bans & restrictions ↗

        Lightning & storms

        Thunderstorm potential uses CAPE (instability, J/kg) plus precipitation signals. Dry lightning is a leading cause of new wildfires, and open water is one of the most dangerous places to be in a storm.

        Visibility & smoke

        Forecast visibility captures fog, rain and haze; in thick wildfire smoke real visibility can drop faster than models show — lean on PM2.5/AQHI. When smoke is heavy: stay indoors with windows closed, run a HEPA purifier or well-fitted N95, and take breaks in cleaner-air spaces. Children, seniors, pregnant people and those with heart/lung conditions are most sensitive.

        About & disclaimer
        EmberWatch combines publicly available environmental and wildfire information to help you understand conditions around your location. EmberWatch is not an emergency service and does not replace official evacuation notices, emergency instructions or information from BC Wildfire Service, EmergencyInfoBC, Environment and Climate Change Canada, or local authorities. Conditions can change rapidly.
