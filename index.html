    📖 Understanding these conditions

    Air quality: AQHI vs. AQI

    Canada uses the Air Quality Health Index (AQHI), a 1–10+ health-risk scale from Environment Canada based on ground-level ozone, NO₂ and fine particulate (PM2.5). 1–3 = low risk (enjoy normal activities), 4–6 = moderate (at-risk people should consider easing outdoor exertion), 7–10 = high (at-risk people avoid strenuous activity outdoors), 10+ = very high (everyone reduce/reschedule outdoor exertion). We also show the U.S. AQI (0–500) because it's more granular at high smoke levels and widely recognized. During wildfire smoke, PM2.5 is the number that matters most.

    Wildfire risk near you

    This blends each fire's size, its stage of control (Out of Control → Being Held → Under Control), whether it's a “fire of note”, and how far it is. A small, contained fire close by stays low; a large, out-of-control fire drives the rating up even from farther away. Fire data is British Columbia only.

    Fire-weather & dryness

    Fires start and spread most easily when fuels are dry — driven by low afternoon humidity, days since meaningful rain, heat and wind. The dryness tag (Moist → Moderate → Dry → Very dry) summarizes that; “· 5d” means 5 days since ≥1 mm of rain. BC's official danger classes run Low → Moderate → High → Extreme; when it's High/Extreme, campfire and open-burning bans are common — check current bans before any burning.

    Lightning & storms

    Thunderstorm potential uses CAPE (atmospheric instability, in J/kg) plus precipitation and storm signals. Roughly: under 1,000 = low, 1,000–2,500 = moderate, above 2,500 = strong potential. Dry lightning is a leading cause of new wildfires, and open water is one of the most dangerous places to be during a storm.

    Visibility

    The forecast visibility captures fog, rain and haze well. In thick wildfire smoke, real visibility can drop faster than the model shows, so treat it as a guide and lean on PM2.5/AQHI as the primary smoke signal. As a rule of thumb, visibility under ~5 km usually means heavy smoke.

    Heat, cooling & UV

    We flag heat by the feels-like high. In BC's interior, sustained daytime highs around the mid-30s °C with warm overnight lows are when Environment Canada issues heat warnings — hydrate, seek shade/AC, and check on neighbours. UV of 8+ is very high: sunscreen, hats and shade, especially for kids. Cold snaps and heavy rain are flagged too.

    Protecting yourself from smoke

    When smoke is heavy: stay indoors with windows closed, run a HEPA/air purifier or a well-fitted N95, avoid strenuous outdoor activity, and take breaks in cleaner-air spaces (libraries, malls, community centres). Children, seniors, pregnant people and those with heart or lung conditions are most sensitive.
